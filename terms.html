Simulated money, not investment advice

Everything in the Market Lab uses simulated money. No real securities are bought or sold, and you can't win or lose real money by trading. The simulator, lessons, information sessions and guest talks are for education. None of it is investment, financial, legal or tax advice, and none of it recommends buying or selling anything. How well you do in the simulation doesn't predict how real investing would go for you.

How the simulation works

The simulation runs from October 1 to November 1, 2026. Every team, including solo entrants, starts with the same amount of simulated cash.

You can trade stocks and ETFs listed on the NYSE, NYSE Arca, NYSE American, Nasdaq, Cboe BZX and TSX, plus cryptocurrencies. Stocks and ETFs trade in whole shares, only while their exchange is open. Leveraged and inverse ETFs aren't available. The competition doesn't allow borrowing or short selling, and those funds are built to do one or the other.

Crypto trades around the clock, seven days a week. You can add any coin that Yahoo Finance prices in Canadian or US dollars, as long as its data passes our checks. Its price has to have updated in the last 10 minutes, at least $250,000 of it has to have traded in the past 24 hours, and there has to be at least four hours of recent price history. Coins priced in US dollars are converted to Canadian dollars at the current exchange rate.

You buy and sell crypto in lots, not single coins. When a coin is first added to the Market Lab, its lot is set to the smallest power of ten of that coin worth at least $10 CAD. If Bitcoin is at $150,000 when it's added, for example, one lot is 0.0001 BTC. For a coin worth a fraction of a cent, a lot can be millions of coins. A coin's lot size never changes after it's added, and you can only trade whole lots.

If a coin stops passing those checks or its price stops coming through, trading in it is paused. You can't buy or sell it until the data recovers, and it counts toward your portfolio at its last known price in the meantime. If one lot of a coin drops below $1, you can't buy more of it, but you can still sell what you have.

We may stop new purchases of ETFs, crypto, or a particular fund or coin, for example if its data looks unreliable. If that happens you keep what you already own, and you can still sell it.

Crypto prices swing far more than most stocks. A coin can lose most of its value in a few hours or days. Small coins with little trading are the most likely to crash, show bad prices or get paused. There's no limit on how much of your portfolio can be in crypto, so how much risk you take is up to you.

Orders come in four types. A market order fills right away at the current price. A limit order waits until the price reaches your limit or better, so a limit buy fills at or below it and a limit sell at or above it. A stop loss sells once the price falls to your stop, then fills at the next price available, which can be lower than the stop if the price drops fast or opens lower than it closed. A take profit sells once the price rises to your target. When you buy, you can attach a take profit and a stop loss to it, and each one is optional. If you set both, whichever fills first cancels the other.

The Market Lab checks waiting orders about once a minute while their market is open, and all day for crypto. An order fills at the price seen during that check, which can differ from the price at the moment your level was crossed. While a buy waits, the cash it would cost is set aside, plus 2% extra on US-dollar listings in case the exchange rate moves. A waiting sell sets aside its shares. That way the same money or shares can't be used twice. A team can have up to 20 waiting orders at a time.

A Day order ends at 4:00 p.m. ET if it hasn't filled (midnight ET for crypto). A Good until cancelled order keeps waiting until it fills or someone on your team cancels it. Every waiting order expires when the competition ends. If an order can't go through when its price is reached, for example because your team no longer has the cash or shares, it's cancelled and the team activity log says why.

Trades use prices from a third-party market data feed. Those prices can be delayed, and now and then they're wrong or missing. If a data error or a software bug produces a trade that couldn't have happened in a real market, we may correct or reverse it.

Winning and prizes

Teams are ranked by total portfolio value, meaning cash plus the market value of holdings, as recorded by the Market Lab when the simulation closes on November 1, 2026. Prizes are in Canadian dollars:

  • First place: $500
  • Second place: $200
  • Third place: $100

Each prize is split evenly among the members of the winning team. If teams tie, we add up the prizes for the places they share and split that total evenly between them.

After the simulation closes we'll announce the results and email the winning teams to arrange payment. A winner who doesn't reply within 14 days of that email may lose their share, and we may award it to the next team in the rankings. Prizes can't be transferred or swapped for something else.

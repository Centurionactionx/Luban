What students practised

Students with varying levels of investing experience worked through the core parts of a strong stock pitch. Advanced valuation was optional; judges were asked to consider clarity, effort, and the skill shown in the case.

  • Qualitative research

    Study the company’s business model, competitive position, and wider industry trends.

  • Financial analysis

    Read financial statements and use basic ratios to give the recommendation evidence.

  • Judge questions

    Explain the reasoning live and respond to three minutes of questions from the panel.

  • Team presentation

    Shape one clear narrative with a five-person team and deliver the pitch through slides.

The ten-minute pitch

Clear thinking mattered more than advanced modelling. The presentation needed to connect the thesis, evidence, and recommendation before the judges opened the floor.

7 minutes
Present the company, evidence, thesis, and buy, sell, or hold recommendation.
3 minutes
Answer questions and defend the reasoning behind the team’s case.
Delivery
Slides presented live through Zoom.

Published participation notes

These were the stated requirements and logistics for teams taking part in the 2026 competition.

  • Team size

    Teams were built toward five members. Individuals and incomplete teams could be matched. Teams under five were asked to email jsun.luban@gmail.com before the deadline.

  • Attendance

    Teams were required at the opening ceremony, closing ceremony, and their own presentation slot. Watching other pitches was optional and expected to be respectful.

  • Timeslots

    Presentation times were scheduled to be emailed to team addresses on the morning of April 5. Scheduling questions went to jsun.luban@gmail.com.

How the simulation worked

Luban’s Economics Competition placed teams inside a live mock economy. Students had to turn classroom concepts into decisions, react to other teams, and then examine the economic patterns created by the game.

  • Resource management

    Allocate limited resources and adjust budgeting decisions as conditions inside the simulation change.

  • Active negotiation

    Trade with other teams, secure needed components, and look for agreements that create value.

  • Economic debrief

    Connect the game to scarcity, supply and demand, inflation, negotiation, and strategic behaviour.

  • Published prizes

    The 2026 event materials stated that cash prizes were part of the competition; amounts were not published.

Published participation notes

These details summarize the audience, team formation, and technology requirements shown for the 2026 event.

  • Eligibility

    Any high school or university student could take part. The rules were intended to be taught at the beginning, so prior coursework was not required.

  • Team matching

    Teams were made up of two to four players. Students without a full team could register individually and be paired before the event.

  • Platform

    The simulation required a stable internet connection and a laptop or desktop capable of running the simulation interface and Zoom together.

  • Event cost

    The published competition was free and online, allowing students to participate from different locations.
